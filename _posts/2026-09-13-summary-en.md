## [Axonet: RMN Aggregation Democratizes Retail Media Access for Advertisers](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMirgFBVV95cUxOTVVRR1ZEUVQ3Mk9MZlhHSlpHemt2X21ncmc3NV90OXM4UGkzQ1lQZGtkUFpwaXhyT3c5UWNDOEdqRTE4OXpFV3pYaU9FTzhWQWlBdkJObi1YRk96YUY4NEhBcDZYNGxtU29Jd2FESWU1VkJsU09qYV83dmpDOHJRTUxQYjNQWWdhbkYyaWp6alFIb0ZJM1RJT1hEWGo3bHdYbW4tUzdSQmlJM1lYeUE?oc=5) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Axonet's Patrick Raycroft highlights that the aggregation of Retail Media Networks (RMNs) is significantly democratizing access to retail media for advertisers. This development allows more brands, including smaller ones, to leverage advertising opportunities within retailer platforms that were previously fragmented. This development is significant because it broadens access to retail media, potentially transforming media buying strategies for brands and agencies by simplifying the process of reaching consumers directly on retailer platforms. It makes a crucial advertising channel more available to a wider range of advertisers, fostering greater competition and innovation. The aggregation of RMNs aims to streamline the complex landscape of individual retailer advertising platforms, offering advertisers a more unified and efficient way to manage campaigns across multiple networks. This approach helps overcome the challenge of fragmented data and disparate interfaces that often characterize direct RMN engagements, improving overall campaign effectiveness.

**Background**: Retail Media Networks (RMNs) are advertising platforms operated by retailers, allowing brands to place ads directly on the retailer's websites, apps, and sometimes in-store. These networks leverage first-party customer data to offer highly targeted advertising opportunities, making them a rapidly growing segment of the digital advertising landscape. RMNs enable brands to reach consumers at the point of purchase, influencing buying decisions directly.

Google has admitted that its Search Console currently lacks adequate reporting for organic search positioning within its new AI-powered search results, specifically for AI search positioning data. This admission is significant for SEO professionals and businesses as it reveals a critical measurement gap in understanding content performance within Google's evolving AI-powered search landscape, making it harder to optimize for these new results. The inadequacy specifically refers to reporting on "AI search positioning data" for SEO, meaning webmasters cannot accurately track how their content ranks or appears within the AI Overviews or Search Generative Experience.

**Background**: Google Search Console is a free service that helps website owners monitor their site's performance in Google Search, identify issues, and optimize for better visibility. Google's AI-powered search results, known as Search Generative Experience (SGE) or AI Overviews, integrate AI-generated responses at the top of search results, aiming to provide quick answers using large language models like Gemini 3.
