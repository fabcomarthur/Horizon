## [Retail Media Confronts Conflict in Ad Performance Measurement](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMimAFBVV95cUxQSmxWaDViU3BqZUNnWkJLOTNNd1F4MHZLZ21wdHg3QUdvVDlQc1R4NUVjc3BhQnl2dDk4UnZ5UHZaMEx4NlRLNWlVVmp3VlAwSmxVZ1FvT2V3T19qbjd5UzNYOFphUkRVNkZRcHc5TE1mdDhLbkZXVTJKMFp3NEs4NHN3U01JcUd4RGxKN2R4cDFtUXNWc0U4Zg?oc=5) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The article highlights a significant challenge in retail media: retailers act as both ad hosts and performance reporters, creating an inherent conflict of interest in transparent measurement and attribution. This situation, dubbed the "grading-your-own-homework problem," directly impacts the reliability of ad performance data. This conflict directly impacts how brands buy and measure ads on retail platforms, undermining trust and making it difficult to accurately assess return on investment across the rapidly growing retail media sector. Without independent verification, advertisers struggle to confidently allocate budgets and optimize campaigns. The core issue is that retailers, who benefit from ad revenue, are responsible for reporting the effectiveness of those ads, leading to concerns about biased data and the lack of independent verification for ad performance metrics. This "grading-your-own-homework" scenario makes it hard for advertisers to trust the reported attribution and understand the true impact of their spending.
